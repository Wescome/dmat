# DMAT research — instances per audience and discipline

## 1. Principle: one core, many instances

DMAT has one research core and many readerships. The core is fixed and versioned:

- **The calculus**: coordination system, dependency, protocol, environment, arm; operational vs epistemic adequacy; axioms A1–A6; Theorem 1 (indistinguishability), Theorem 2 (ceiling on sound judgment), Theorem 3 (composition requires distinguishability), Theorem 4 (conditional preservation under refinement).
- **The executable models** (v0.3): eight families, five arms, 36 profiles, exact metrics, determinacy by exhaustive search, independent arms with the differential check and adjudication record.
- **The hub paper** WP-2026-DM-01, which states the whole program once.

An *instance* is a paper (or article, or talk) for one discipline. It restates the core in that discipline's vocabulary, carries the part of the core that discipline can evaluate, and adds the new work that discipline requires before it will accept the contribution. Four rules hold for every instance:

1. **Distinct contribution.** Each instance makes a contribution the others do not; restating the hub for a new venue is duplicate publication. The new work listed per instance is what makes it publishable on its own.
2. **Numbers come from the models.** Every figure in every instance is generated by `models/` at a tagged version, as the hub's are; an instance cites the version it used.
3. **The hub is cited, not repeated.** Instances cite WP-2026-DM-01 (and its arXiv version once posted) for the parts they summarize.
4. **The research guideline governs content and production**; the venue governs the template (IEEEtran for IEEE venues, ACM `acmart`, Springer LNCS, journal house styles). Decision D2.

## 2. Instances

Each entry: the audience's question, the core reframed in its terms, what it carries from the core, the new work it requires, and the venue class.

### I-1 Formal methods and concurrency theory

- **Question the audience asks:** what is provable about observability and compliance judgment in coordination calculi, and how does it relate to known results on knowledge and monitorability?
- **Reframing:** the ceiling theorem as a characterization of sound partial compliance judgment over an observation equivalence weighted by an environment; composition and refinement results as closure properties of determinacy.
- **Carries:** §III–IV of the hub, Appendix A, the P2 and P3 tables as witnesses.
- **New work:** (a) mechanized proofs of Theorems 1–4 and Propositions 1–2 in a proof assistant (Lean 4 or Coq), extracted from the Racket definitions; (b) generalization from finite supports to infinite and probabilistic environments (measurable observation maps); (c) symbolic determinacy for timed environments (timed-automaton semantics instead of enumerated delays); (d) the precise relation to knowledge-based programs and to three-valued monitorability.
- **Venues:** COORDINATION or FORTE (DisCoTec), CONCUR; journal version in Logical Methods in Computer Science or Theoretical Computer Science.

### I-2 Runtime verification

- **Question:** when can a monitor issue a definitive verdict, and how should monitors be compared?
- **Reframing:** the canonical judge j* as the optimal sound monitor for a given observation interface and fault environment; coverage–risk reporting (supported, lucky, false, abstained-ok, abstained-miss) as a monitor-evaluation methodology.
- **Carries:** Theorem 2, Corollary 1, the class definitions, the hybrid's evidentiary rules E1–E3 as monitor design rules.
- **New work:** synthesis of j* from a protocol interface and an environment specification (not by enumeration of a finite support); evaluation against existing RV tools on DMBench traces.
- **Venues:** RV (International Conference on Runtime Verification); journal version in Formal Methods in System Design.

### I-3 Multiagent systems and normative MAS

- **Question:** do commitment-based protocols deliver accountability, and what must be added for an agent to know whether a commitment was discharged?
- **Reframing:** commitments give responsibility continuity (H1) but not evidence soundness; E1–E3 as an evidentiary semantics layered on commitments; the composition result as the general form of Baldoni et al.'s compositional-verification problem.
- **Carries:** the delegation, replacement, agreement and composition families; H1, H4, H6; the ablation.
- **New work:** (a) additional arms written in the community's own formalisms: BSPL information protocols and Cupid/Azorus-style commitment specifications, enacted by agents rather than encoded as judges; (b) LLM-agent enactment of the protocols, judged by the same evaluator; (c) a norm-evidence vocabulary aligned with the community's commitment ontologies.
- **Venues:** AAMAS (main track), EMAS, COINE workshop; journal version in JAAMAS.

### I-4 Programming languages: session types and choreographies

- **Question:** what does conformance to a global type guarantee about the business dependency the protocol serves?
- **Reframing:** conformance ≠ adequacy; the choreography arm's failures (timeouts on slow but timely replies, type-level conformance over unfitting payloads, facts outside the projection) as precise gaps; refinement types and timed session types as candidate remedies, measured.
- **Carries:** the CHOR arm across families, adjudication causes A1 and K1, the deadline and agreement families.
- **New work:** CHOR arms generated from real global types with an MPST toolchain (Scribble/νScr or a Rust or OCaml MPST library), with and without refinement types and timed types; measurement of how much each extension closes the gap.
- **Venues:** PLACES workshop, ECOOP, ESOP; COORDINATION as an alternative.

### I-5 Software engineering: empirical method and benchmark

- **Question:** is the benchmark valid, reproducible, and does its method transfer?
- **Reframing:** executable models as the reference semantics for a benchmark; the independent-authorship differential protocol as a validity procedure; adjudication against a written specification as a method that found twelve reference errors.
- **Carries:** §V and §VII of the hub, the specifications, the adjudication record, the reproducibility package.
- **New work:** (a) the arms implemented over real transports and stores (for example a durable-workflow engine, a message broker, a replicated store), judged by the same evaluator with the models as oracle; (b) independent authorship by separate human teams, replicating the differential check (decision D3); (c) a preregistered primary endpoint and clustered bootstrap analysis for the implementation runs, as the framework's validity design requires.
- **Venues:** ICSE or FSE research track with artifact evaluation; Empirical Software Engineering (registered-report track) or TOSEM.

### I-6 Distributed systems design

- **Question:** what should a protocol designer build so that participants can know outcomes under faults?
- **Reframing:** the design rules of §X of the hub (compute the ceiling, separate reports from evidence, watermark records, retain responsibility, distinguish remote stages at every relay, judge by trusted asserted time, fence what expires, make Indeterminate first-class) as systems principles with their fault evidence.
- **Carries:** §X, the deadline, reservation, handoff and external families.
- **New work:** the ceiling computed for two or three real protocols in use (a lease service, a payment or order workflow, a replicated log's read path), with the interface change that raises it.
- **Venues:** HotOS (position), PaPoC workshop, Middleware or EuroSys for the full version.

### I-7 Information systems, BPM and organization science

- **Question:** which coordination mechanism should an organization choose for which dependency?
- **Reframing:** Malone–Crowston coordination theory with adequacy made relative to dependency, environment and evidence; the protocol-selection problem (framework contribution C4) as constrained optimization with the evidence constraint given exactly by determinable mass.
- **Carries:** the dependency taxonomy, the family-by-family findings, the separation of operational and epistemic adequacy.
- **New work:** (a) the selection method itself: utility and cost models, validated on held-out scenario families; (b) field cases from at least two organizations' coordination processes, coded as dependencies and mechanisms.
- **Venues:** BPM conference, CAiSE; theory article in Information Systems Research or Organization Science.

### I-8 Knowledge representation and ontology

- **Question:** can the coordination, interaction, behavioural, normative, authority and epistemic perspectives be integrated without semantic collapse?
- **Reframing:** the unified metamodel U = ⟨C, I, B, N, A, E, L⟩ with the calculus deciding which judgments belong to operational semantics rather than to OWL entailment.
- **Carries:** the framework's Part III, the v0.1 ontology modules and SHACL shapes, A3 (semantic non-equivalence).
- **New work:** a versioned OWL/SHACL release with PROV-O alignment; competency questions answered against DMBench run records (the 18,000 sampled runs) exported as RDF; the linkage rules stated as operational rules, not entailments.
- **Venues:** FOIS; journal version in Applied Ontology or the Semantic Web Journal.

### I-9 Law, accountability and AI governance

- **Question:** what can an auditor or regulator establish about whether an automated or multi-party process met its obligations?
- **Reframing:** reports are not evidence (E1); records without completeness bounds cannot prove absence (E2); accountability gaps arise from releasing responsibility before acceptance (A4, H1); there is a computable limit on what any audit over a given record can establish (Theorem 2).
- **Carries:** the evidentiary rules, false-completion results, accountability gap results, the worked example.
- **New work:** mapping to audit and record-keeping obligations in a chosen regime (for example the EU AI Act's logging and traceability provisions, or clinical-handoff documentation rules), and an analysis of which obligations are epistemically adequate under realistic record faults.
- **Venues:** FAccT, AIES, ICAIL; journal version in Artificial Intelligence and Law.

### I-10 Health informatics

- **Question:** can a referring organization establish that a care transition was accepted and initiated?
- **Reframing:** the handoff and delegation families as care-transition and subcontracted-care models; the ceiling as the limit on what referral tracking can establish from current interoperability messages.
- **Carries:** the handoff, delegation and composition families; the worked example.
- **New work:** the families instantiated over standard referral messaging (for example HL7 FHIR `ServiceRequest`/`Task` workflows) with the faults observed in practice; the interface additions that raise determinable mass, stated as implementation guidance.
- **Venues:** AMIA Annual Symposium; journal version in JAMIA or Journal of Biomedical Informatics.

### I-11 Practitioner article

- **Question:** what should engineers building multi-party workflows do differently?
- **Reframing:** the design rules with one worked example and no formalism.
- **Carries:** §IX–X of the hub.
- **New work:** none beyond writing; it follows I-6 so the rules carry real-protocol evidence.
- **Venues:** ACM Queue, IEEE Software, Communications of the ACM (Practice).

## 4. Shared assets each phase maintains

- `models/`: the reference semantics; every new arm, family or environment enters through it, with tests, and every instance's tables are generated from it.
- `specs/`: paradigm and family specifications; every new arm family gets a specification before code, so independent authorship stays possible.
- `paper/`: the hub. It is revised only when the core changes (a new theorem, a new family), not for instance-specific results.
- A per-instance directory (`instances/I-n/`) holding that paper's source, built to the research guideline with the venue's template.
